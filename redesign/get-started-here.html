What happens, and when

A typical year for local universities. Dates shift each year, so always check each university's admissions page. Overseas universities run on their own timelines.

  1. Mid-February
    A-Level results are released. University open houses run around the same time, so go and talk to students and staff.
    Open house dates
  2. February to March
    Applications open. You apply to each university separately, one application per university.
  3. March to May
    Interviews and selection tests for courses that need them (Medicine, Law, Architecture and more).
  4. May to July
    Offers are released. Accept the one you want. Appeals open if you have no offer or missed your course.
  5. May to August
    Matriculation, orientation and the start of your studies.

How local applications work

Three things to understand before you submit.

Apply to each, separately

There's no single portal: you submit one application per university, wait for offers (roughly May to July) and confirm the one you want.

No offer, or not your course? You can appeal, but policies differ. NUS suggests appealing to a different course, realistically.

Ranking your choices matters

At NUS you get up to 2.5 bonus points for a first-choice non-interview course, enough to overtake a higher-scoring second-choice applicant.

Medicine, Dentistry and Law must be ranked 1st or 2nd to be shortlisted. Architecture, Industrial Design and Nursing must be in your top 3.

Read the IGP as a guide

The Indicative Grade Profile shows last year's intake: useful, but not a promise. Cut-offs move with demand, and some courses need specific H2 subjects.

Missed a cut-off? Aptitude-Based Admissions at NUS, NTU and SMU can still consider your portfolio, experience and interest.

Six myths, busted

What Victorians worry about every year. Tap a card to see the truth.

Not true

Local universities hold places for you during NS, so apply this year with your cohort. Fees lock at this year's rate, and you can re-apply later if you change your mind.

Not true

SUTD's intake is small (about 500), and it looks at the whole applicant. It interviews applicants and weighs interests, leadership and fit alongside points.

Use it as a guide

Cut-offs change with demand, and NUS, NTU and SMU all run Aptitude-Based Admissions. With aptitude, a strong portfolio or real involvement, you can still apply.

Not necessarily

You'll be excluded from some courses (Law has a GP requirement at NUS and SMU), but you can usually still apply to general courses.

Not necessarily

SIT and SUSS need a minimum of 2 H2 passes (plus a GP pass in the same sitting), against 3 H2 passes at NUS, NTU, SMU and SUTD.

You have options

Resitting is only one route. SIT and SUSS need just 2 H2 passes and look at the whole applicant. Polytechnics offer advanced standing.

Your questions answered

Which university should I apply to?

There's no harm applying to a few to buy time before deciding. Weigh scholarships, how specialised the course is, teaching reputation and hall life.

Which course should I choose?

Balance your interests, ability, job prospects and grades. Don't just continue your A-Level subjects: universities offer courses that don't exist at A-Level.

I scored well and take H2 Chemistry. Should I apply for Medicine?

Think carefully, especially if you'd never considered it before results day. Medicine is a calling: five hard years, shorter holidays, high cost and real personal sacrifice.

Does the ranking of my choices matter?

Yes, and differently at each university. NUS adds up to 2.5 bonus points to a first-choice non-interview course.

Does failing Mother Tongue mean I can't apply?

No. Universities have other ways to meet the Mother Tongue requirement, and you can usually be admitted provisionally.

Should I retake the A-Levels as a private candidate?

It's harder than it looks: private candidates don't tend to do better. Weigh the alternatives before committing.

Can I retake just one subject?

NUS, NTU and SMU count GP and 3 H2 subjects from a single sitting, so to improve those you must retake all your H2 content subjects and GP together.

Thinking about Medicine?

Good A-Level results alone aren't a reason to apply. Medicine asks for care, compassion and sacrifice across five demanding years. If it's truly your calling, apply with your eyes open and get the mechanics right.

  • Rank Medicine 1st or 2nd at NUS to be considered; a 2nd choice must sit behind another interview course.
  • Nominate teachers for referee reports early. For NTU Medicine, one of the two must be your Civics Tutor.
  • Referee deadlines are shown only to you and can differ by applicant. Tell your teachers clearly.
  • Submit the NUS Medicine portfolio separately on the NUS Medicine website, as well as your application.

References, NS and paying for it

Practical things that trip people up every year.

Asking for references and certified copies

  • Ask tutors early and politely, with reasonable deadlines. Give them everything they need for the secure upload link.
  • Don't make late requests, ask for open letters of recommendation, or request references that aren't required.
  • Applying for scholarships too? Deadlines often overlap, so give tutors extra notice. Scholarship timelines
  • Certified copies: the General Office can stamp "certified true". Bring originals and photocopies in person.

National Service and the money side

  • Apply this year with your cohort, even if you're serving NS. Places are held and fees lock at this year's rate.
  • Change your mind during NS? You can re-apply later, but fees rise each year.
  • Financial aid is offered at every local university. University Access guarantees 100% tuition coverage for Singaporeans with the greatest need: universityaccess.sg.
